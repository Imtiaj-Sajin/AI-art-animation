# Episode 01 — The Vanishing at Flannan Isles (research notes)

Fact-checked 2026-10-28. Everything said in the narration must trace back to a line here.

## Verified facts

| Fact | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Flannan Isles, ~20 miles (32 km) west of the Isle of Lewis, Outer Hebrides, Scotland. Lighthouse on the largest island, Eilean Mòr. | [1], [2] |
| Lighthouse | Designed by David Alan Stevenson for the Northern Lighthouse Board (NLB). **First lit 7 December 1899.** | [2] |
| Keepers | **James Ducat** (Principal Keeper), **Thomas Marshall** (assistant), **Donald McArthur** ("the Occasional": a temporary/local keeper standing in for William Ross, who was on sick leave). | [2], [3] |
| Light seen dark | **15 Dec 1900**: steamer *Archtor* (Philadelphia → Leith) saw the light was not lit in poor weather. Reported when she docked at Leith on **18 Dec**. | [2], [3] |
| Relief delayed | Relief vessel *Hesperus* (captain **Jim Harvie**) couldn't sail on **20 Dec** due to weather; reached the island around **noon, 26 Dec**: 11 days after the light was last seen dark. | [2], [3] |
| Arrival | No flag on the flagstaff, no provision boxes left at the landing, no one came to meet the boat. Harvie sounded the horn and set off a flare; no reply. | [2], [3] |
| Telegram | Harvie: *"A dreadful accident has happened at the Flannans. The three keepers, Ducat, Marshall and the Occasional have disappeared from the Island..."* | [2] |
| What Joseph Moore found | Relief keeper Joseph Moore went up alone. Entrance gate and main door **closed**. Beds **unmade/empty**. **Clock stopped** (unwound). Fire not lit. Lamps **cleaned and refilled**. Moore: *"The kitchen utensils were all very clean, which is a sign that it must be after dinner some time they left."* | [2], [3] |
| Last record | Last entries on the log slate were for **15 December 1900**. | [3] |
| Oilskins | One set of oilskins was still there, so one man went out without them. Muirhead's report says **McArthur** left in his **shirtsleeves**, in heavy rain. | [2], [3] |
| West landing damage | Box (of mooring/landing ropes) secured in a crevice ~**33–34 m (110 ft)** above sea level was broken, its contents (ropes) strewn. **Iron railings bent**; the railway wrenched from its concrete; a rock of **over a ton** displaced; turf torn up **over 60 m** above sea level. | [2] |
| Muirhead quote | Damage was *"difficult to believe unless actually seen."* | [2] |
| Official conclusion | Superintendent **Robert Muirhead** (NLB; he had recruited all three men) concluded Ducat and Marshall went to the west landing to secure the box of ropes, McArthur followed, and an exceptionally large sea swept them away. | [2], [3] |
| Bodies | Never found. | [1], [3] |
| Automation | Lighthouse **automated 28 September 1971**. No permanent residents since. | [1], [2] |

## Myths (do NOT state as fact; the episode debunks them)

- **Uneaten meal + overturned chair**: from Wilfrid Wilson Gibson's 1912 poem *Flannan Isle*, not the record. [2]
- **Dramatic logbook entries** (severe storm, "Ducat very quiet", McArthur crying, praying, *"Storm ended, sea calm. God is over all."*): later inventions; Mike Dash's *Fortean Times* investigation found them fictional. [2], [4]
- Canary in a cage: appears in some retellings; unverified, so don't use it.

## Interpretation used in the script (clearly framed as theory)

McArthur may have seen an exceptionally large wave approaching and run out (without his oilskins) to warn the other two. This is a reconstruction consistent with Muirhead's conclusion, not a documented fact. The script says "may have" and "perhaps".
